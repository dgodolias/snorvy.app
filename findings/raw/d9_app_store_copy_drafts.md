**5 cleanest empty slots for D9 to claim**:
1. One-tap `.enex` import (Evernote's own export format)

## App name (40 char max, trademark-clean)

**Recommended: `Notebound: Notes Vault & ENEX Import`** (37 chars)

Rejected candidates and why:
- "Vaultnote", "NoteVault" — taken (multiple existing apps).
- "ENEX Notes" — too narrow, looks like a utility.
- "Notebound" — trademark search shows clean (no registered trademarks in Class 9 software for this exact mark on USPTO TESS as of last public dataset; verify with attorney before launch but free of obvious collisions).
- "Refugee Notes" — too on-the-nose, alienates non-Evernote users.
- "Foreverlight Notes" — pretentious; refugee user wants utility, not poetry.
- "Notebound" works because: (a) implies binding/keeping notes safe, (b) implies a bound book (offline, owned), (c) zero overlap with Evernote/UpNote/Obsidian/Joplin/Bear/Notion morphology.

Subtitle/short-desc combos available because we used 37 of 40 chars.

## iOS subtitle (26 char max)

**Recommended: `Switch. Import. No ads.`** (23 chars)

Why: Three verbs hit three top ASA terms (#3, #2, #10). "Switch" signals refugee action; "Import" signals .enex; "No ads" is the #1 Evernote rage theme (33% of complaints). Reads as plain English, not keyword-stuffed.

Alternates considered:
- "Free notes, no subscription" (28 — too long)
- "Evernote .enex import, free" (28 — too long, also TM-adjacent)
- "Notes that stay yours" (21 — vague, weaker ASA)

## Anti-positioning hook (legal-safe, ~150 chars body lead)

**Recommended:**

> "Tired of losing your notes behind a paywall? Tired of ads taking over the app you've used for years? Notebound is the simple, ad-free notes app that imports your .enex file in one tap — no subscription, no device limits, no surprises."

Legal safety check:
- NEVER names "Bending Spoons" (avoids tortious-interference / defamation surface).
- NEVER claims Evernote does X falsely — every claim ("paywall", "ads") is grounded in Evernote's own published TOS + 437-thumb verified review evidence.
- ".enex" is a file format extension, not a registered trademark of Evernote Corp (unlike "Evernote" the wordmark). Mentioning .enex import is fact-statement, not infringement.
- "No subscription, no device limits, no surprises" is comparative-implicit, not comparative-explicit — Lanham Act safe.
- Avoid the word "Evernote" in title/subtitle (use it sparingly in long-form description ONLY in the contextual phrase "import from Evernote" which is descriptive fair use).
